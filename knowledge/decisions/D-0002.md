# D-0002 — The AzimuthLocation protocol

Accepted 2026-09-24. Rusty: "I bet we could make some kind of AzimuthLocation protocol and then
have the atlas implement it."

The bar does not know what a village, a camp or a landmark is. It knows **places**, and asks
**providers** for them. The protocol is `com.chunkworks.azimuth.api`, JDK-only, in the domain
source set and in the shipped jar, so a mod compiles against the jar and no client class crosses
the boundary.

- **`AzimuthLocation(provider, id, name, dimension, x, y, z, icon, color)`**: immutable. AF: a
  place with a provider-owned identity, a position in a dimension, and how to draw it (the item
  whose sprite marks it, the RGB tint of the dot beneath). RI: provider, dimension and icon are
  namespaced identifiers; id 1..160 characters; name 1..64 without control or formatting codes;
  coordinates finite and within the world; colour 24-bit. `key()` = (provider, id). The
  validators are the ones Magical Map's `Location` uses, written once in `Identifiers`.
- **`AzimuthViewer(player, dimension, x, y, z)`**: who is asking and where they stand. Azimuth
  tells a provider nothing else about the viewer on purpose: what a viewer carries or may see is
  the provider's own business to check, on the server, by the player's id. That is what keeps
  "atlas places only while you carry an atlas" the atlas's rule, not the bar's.
- **`AzimuthProvider`**: `id()`, `bearings(viewer, range)`. Spec: called on the server thread once
  per cycle per viewer; answer with an immutable, bounded list, in the viewer's dimension and
  within range where that is cheap to know; never load or generate chunks, never mutate the
  world, leave out what the viewer may not see. The host culls to the dimension and range anyway,
  caps nearest first, and sends. A provider that throws is logged once and skipped until it
  answers again; the others keep answering.
- **`AzimuthProviders`**: the ordered registry; duplicate ids refused; register from a mod
  constructor. Azimuth registers `azimuth:compass` and `azimuth:death` first.
- **Players are not a provider**: the bar shows them itself, with rules of its own (D-0001), and a
  provider that listed players would double them.
- **The fade rule travels with the payload** (the players' fade distances and floor, the places'
  range and band), so a client needs no copy of the server's config and a server change reaches
  every client on the next cycle.

Rejected: a NeoForge event fired at server start to gather providers (Magical Map's pattern):
a static registry is simpler, mods register from their constructors as they do for the Village
protocol, and a provider needs no server handle since the viewer's id reaches the player through
the running server; client-side providers (a compass is readable on the client, but one protocol
with one host is worth more than saving a packet); a per-place range override (the server's
range is the bar's rule; a provider that wants a place seen from farther is asking for a
different bar).

Verified by the GameTests (a test provider answering in range, beyond range and in another
dimension beside a throwing one; the compass and death providers over a real inventory) and by
the booth (a provider's bell, camp, chest and a lodestone compass drawn, a pickaxe out of range
not).
